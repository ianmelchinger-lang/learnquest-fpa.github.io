Which statement records it?

Each financial statement answers its own question about a business. A transaction appears on a statement only when that statement’s question gets a yes. You advise Sample Enterprise Manufacturing (SEM), a fictional manufacturer. Eight transactions follow. For each one, answer the three questions.

The income statement answers whether the business earned more than it spent, on an accrual basis: it records revenue when the business earns it and expenses when the business incurs them, whatever day the cash moves. The balance sheet answers what the business owns, owes, and has left for its owners, called equity, on one date. The cash flow statement answers how much cash came in and went out.

Answer all 24 questions to check.

Change the accounting basis

On a cash basis, as this activity defines it, the income statement records revenue and expenses on the day cash moves. Switch the basis and see which transactions change their income statement answer. The other two statements stay the same.

SEM is fictional. All transactions and amounts in this activity are invented for practice.
